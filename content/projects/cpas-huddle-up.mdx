## The Problem

Coaches and athletes wanted a place that felt like a team room rather than a general social feed. Film review, drills and pre-game talk had to live in one mobile app, with a way to go live when the squad needed a huddle in real time.

## What I Built

At StackUp Solutions I built the **React Native frontend** for **CPAS Huddle Up**, a sports coaching community made for a client. Rooms are the core of the app: find clubs by sport, join featured and upcoming sessions, post film and drills, and start **live podcast and film-review sessions over Agora**.

- **Find and join rooms.** Search and browse featured, upcoming and suggested rooms for football, soccer, basketball and more, with member counts.
- **Room feed.** Coach and athlete posts with images, titles and comments: film breakdowns, drills and live announcements.
- **Live sessions.** Start a titled live session, stream audio into the room, and keep a comment thread running while the review goes on.
- **Sign-up and coach onboarding.** Login, a multi-step registration (account, profile, address, coaching record), OTP, and plan and subscription screens.
- **Account and moderation.** Coach profiles by plan, notifications, invites and reporting.

## Key Challenges

**Rooms that feel like clubs.** Finding, joining and posting in a room had to follow the same pattern, so coaches could run a community without learning a new product.

**Live audio next to posts.** Agora sessions sat alongside ordinary film posts. Starting, stopping, titles and comments had to stay clear on patchy mobile connections.

**Working inside a client's product.** The frontend had to match the CPAS Huddle Up brand and show plan-aware profiles while the backend sat with others.

## Results

Shipped to the client in 2023 with room discovery, a social feed and live podcasts over Agora. The product has since been taken offline.

## Stack Decisions

React Native for iOS and Android from one codebase. **Agora** for the live audio, which was less work than a custom WebRTC stack when the need was a room broadcast with comments rather than calls between peers.

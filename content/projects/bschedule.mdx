## The Problem

A business client needed scheduling for two audiences: **staff** running the day's jobs, and **customers** booking appointments. Two separate systems would drift apart, so both had to read and write one schedule.

## What I Built

At StackUp Solutions I built BSchedule as **two React Native apps**, staff and customer, on one **Laravel** backend that holds the scheduling and payment logic.

- **Timeline.** Every job by the hour: time, length, assigned staff, customer and price.
- **Month view.** A coloured dot on each day for every appointment category, with a tag on days that have notes.
- **Customers.** A list you can search by name, with phone and address.
- **Admin tools.** The staff app adds staff management, weekly revenue and disabled dates.
- **New jobs.** Pick staff, category, date, time and duration.
- **Premium.** Later the client wanted a subscription product. I led the **in-app purchase** work, so premium users can add, edit and remove their own appointment categories and change which fields the appointment form shows.
- **Job alerts.** I led the **OneSignal** push notifications that tell staff when a job is assigned to them.

## Key Challenges

**One API, two kinds of user.** Staff needed broad read and write access; customers needed a narrow booking view of the same schedule. Getting the endpoints right up front mattered more than shipping two almost-identical APIs.

**Two apps that feel like one.** Both apps share the same screens, and the staff app adds a More tab for staff, revenue and disabled dates, so a customer and a staff member see the same schedule in the same layout.

## Results

Both apps shipped on iOS and Android. They have since been taken out of the stores.

## Stack Decisions

One Laravel API so staff and customer data could not fork into two versions of the truth. React Native with Redux kept both apps on one codebase and one design, with SQLite on the device, in-app purchases for premium and OneSignal for alerts.

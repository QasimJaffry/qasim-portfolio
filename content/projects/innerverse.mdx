## The Problem

Mood tracking apps feel like forms. Logging should take seconds, and the patterns it builds up should be something you actually want to look at.

## What I Built

Innerverse is a mood journal for Android where every entry becomes a world on your galaxy map. I designed, built and published it solo under Jafrix Systems.

- **Logging flow.** Pick a mood, set its intensity on a dial, tag what's going on, and add it to your cosmos. The entry becomes a world on the map.
- **Patterns.** A month shown as a star chart, a heatmap of mood by time of day and day of week, and a plain-language note on your best days.
- **The Celestial Codex.** Gentle, vivid and profound entries become different worlds, from stars and asteroids to comets, nebulas and supernovas.
- **Subscriptions.** RevenueCat with server-owned entitlements: a webhook reducer covered by automated tests, plus Firestore security-rules tests.

Live on [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=innerverse.app).

## Key Challenges

**A galaxy that stays readable.** The map is drawn with Skia and has to work whether it holds ten logs or a year of them, so the year view gathers each month into its own cloud.

**Premium that the server decides.** Plus is sold through RevenueCat. Premium status is written only by a webhook handler on the server; the app can read it but never write it. The handler ignores replayed, out-of-order and test events, and it has unit tests and Firestore rules tests.

**Private by design.** Journal text stays on the user's side: no entry text is sent to any AI service in the shipped app.

## Results

Live on Google Play as a Jafrix Systems product I own end to end, from design to store release.

## Stack Decisions

Expo and React Native on the New Architecture, with Skia and Reanimated for the galaxy, Zustand for state and Firebase (Auth, Firestore, Storage, Functions) behind it. Subscription entitlements are owned on the server, driven by RevenueCat webhooks.

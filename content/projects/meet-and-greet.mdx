## The Problem

Video and voice calls on mobile are easy to demo on good Wi-Fi and hard to ship. Signalling, connection setup and people joining or leaving a group call all have to survive weak networks and mid-range phones.

## What I Built

At StackUp Solutions I built Meet & Greet, a React Native calling app:

- **Presence and dial-by-ID.** See who is online and call them by ID, without digging through contacts.
- **One-to-one calls.** Video and voice over **WebRTC**.
- **Group calls.** People can join and leave, and the call renegotiates as the room changes.
- **Signalling.** **Socket.IO** on a **Node.js** server carries offers, answers, ICE candidates and joins and leaves, and reconnects when a phone's network drops.

## Key Challenges

**Group calls.** Joining, leaving and renegotiating add work that one-to-one calls don't have, and the Socket.IO state has to stay in step with WebRTC on every phone in the call.

**Weak devices and weak networks.** The app was tuned for mid-range phones and poor connections rather than a fast office network.

## Results

One-to-one and group video and voice calls working over WebRTC, with signalling built for mobile networks.

## Stack Decisions

WebRTC carries the audio and video between phones, with STUN and TURN for difficult networks. Socket.IO handles signalling because its reconnection behaviour matters more on mobile than a bare WebSocket.
